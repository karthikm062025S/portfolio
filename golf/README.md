# golf

The side-view golf hole on the site is a small Rust simulation compiled to WebAssembly: a real-unit ball flight (drag, Magnus lift, spin decay) over a generated heightfield, a bounce and roll model, a breadth-first solver that sets par, and a replay code that re-simulates a round in the visitor's browser and says whether the result reproduces. The crate has no dependencies, no allocator and no transcendental functions inside the step, so the same quantized inputs give the same bits on the native test host and in the browser. Every number in `REPORT.md` is generated by the tests; the figures quoted here are copied from it, from the constants in `src/sim.rs`, or from the sources named at the end.

## What it is

- `src/sim.rs`: constants, the force function, the fixed-step integrator, the swept ground crossing, the bounce map, rolling, the cup, the hole generator.
- `src/solve.rs`: the par solver (breadth-first over rest positions, resumable in slices) and the generate-solve-regenerate loop.
- `src/game.rs`: one course: ticks, telemetry, the chained state hash, the shot log, the replay code and its untrusted parser.
- `src/lib.rs`: the wasm exports, one-line wrappers over one static `Game` (plus a second one for replay verification).
- `src/tests.rs`: the test ladder; `golden.txt`: per-seed state hashes from the native host; `check.mjs`: the same script through the built wasm in node; `build.sh`: build, size gate, copy, check.

## The model and its limits

Flight is a point mass with quadratic drag, Magnus lift and exponential spin decay, integrated by semi-implicit Euler at 120 Hz in f64:

```
u  = (vx - wind, vy)                 relative air velocity
S  = clamp(w R / |u|, -0.6, 0.6)     spin ratio; w > 0 is backspin
Cd = 0.23 + 0.3 S^2
Cl = 0.25 S / (|S| + 0.05)
a  = -K |u| (Cd u + Cl u_perp) + g   K = rho A / (2 m)
dw = -2.0e-5 (|u| / R) w
```

- The four aerodynamic constants (0.23, 0.3, 0.25, 0.05) are a fit, not a measurement. They were chosen to reproduce three published tour carries (driver, 7-iron, pitching wedge) and are checked against one independent landing measurement: a driver's landing speed, descent angle and spin, three values the fit never saw. The shape of the curves follows published wind-tunnel ranges, but those papers were not read directly; see Sources.
- Validated band: 46-76 m/s ball speed, spin ratio 0.07-0.45. In the game the ball launches at up to 32 m/s. The game runs the same fit where it is extrapolated, including the critical Reynolds regime below about 35 m/s where real balls show higher drag and can show reverse Magnus. Neither effect is modelled; the ball carries a little too far on slow shots.
- The production integrator falls short of an RK4 f64 reference at dt = 1/4000 s by 0.27-0.53 % of carry on the reference shots (`REPORT.md`, first table). The fitted coefficients are uncertain by several percent (the tour carries are matched to within 4 %, `REPORT.md`, second table), an order of magnitude more than the integrator error, so no higher-order integrator.
- Bounce is the rigid-body Coulomb map of Biber et al. 2023 (their eq. 5) with per-surface restitution and friction. The authors' own fit of that plain rigid map on turf is off by about a third (32-35 % in their Table 3), and no green or sand data exists; the surface table is tuned so the model's own landings roll out on the scale of a 25-50 m hole. It is a game model with fitted constants, not bounce physics.
- Rolling is a constant deceleration along the polyline (green: Stimp 10 by the stimpmeter formula; fairway and sand: tuned) plus the aerodynamic force projected on the tangent. A ball rests when it has been slower than 0.05 m/s for 30 steps and rolling resistance can hold it, or when it sits in a valley vertex.
- The cup is a capture rule, not a lip-out simulation: a rolling ball crossing the cup centre slower than 1.6 m/s drops; faster, it is kicked (a rolling miss under 4 m/s is dumped to a tap-in). An airborne ball whose centre crosses the green inside the rim, descending steeper than 30 degrees at up to 12 m/s, drops.
- Launch speed tops out at 32 m/s, which carries 50-85 m against holes of 25-50 m, so the top third of the power range overshoots every hole. It is left that way on purpose: the power curve keeps fine control at the low end.
- Not physics at all: the invisible walls at both ends of the hole (restitution 0.3), the water pit that returns the ball to its last rest with one penalty stroke, the 20-stroke cap, the spin settings (0 / 1500 / 3000 / 4500 rpm at launch) and the launch speed curve.

## Determinism and the hash

- Only `+ - * /` and a Newton square root (bit-hack seed, five iterations of plain IEEE operations) run inside the step. No libm, no `sin cos exp pow`; aim comes from a literal table of 65 sines, launch speed from `t * sqrt(sqrt(t))`. The square root is within 1 ulp of the correctly rounded value on 100,000 samples and is the same bits on both hosts; it is not the IEEE `sqrt` instruction.
- The sim advances only in whole 120 Hz ticks. A frame longer than 1/15 s is clamped and its lost ticks counted; at most 8 ticks run per frame. `settle()` is `advance` in a loop; live play, replay verification and the solver share one step function and one 20 s per-shot cap.
- The state hash is FNV-1a 64 over, per hole, every height sample and surface byte, par and wind, then per shot the bit patterns of `x, y, vx, vy, w` at rest plus the stroke count and event bits, chained across shots and holes. What it proves: this build agrees with itself and with the native test host (`golden.txt` versus `check.mjs`). What it does not prove: bit parity on engines other than the one `check.mjs` ran on (V8); Firefox and WebKit were not run.
- Replay code: `G`, version 1, seed, hole count, then per hole the shot count and `(angle, spin << 6 | power)` bytes, then the low 32 bits of the hash after the last hole, base64url without padding. The parser treats the string as untrusted; `replay_verify` returns 1 match, 0 differs, -1 empty or too long, -2 bad character, -3 bad length, -4 bad magic, -5 bad version, -6 hole or shot count out of range, -7 trailing bytes.

## The wasm surface

All exports are plain functions over one static game; integers are `u32`/`i32`, floats `f64`; `memory` is exported and the host reads the buffers through it.

- `new_course(seed)`: a fresh nine-hole course, nothing prepared. `hole_prepare(k)` begins hole `k` (0..8); `hole_solve(max_shots)` continues the par search and returns par (2..5) when ready, `-1` while more work remains; `hole_start()` makes the prepared hole the playing hole (1 ok, 0 not prepared); `restart()` tees the playing hole up again.
- `shoot(angle_q, power_q, spin_q)`: 1 accepted, 0 rejected. Angle 0..255 (0 is +x, 64 up), power 0..63, spin 0..3. `advance(dt_ms)` steps in whole ticks and returns the event bits since the last call (1 land, 2 land in sand, 4 water, 8 holed, 16 wall, 32 rim, 64 rest, 128 hole over at 20 strokes); `settle()` runs the shot to its end the same way. `preview(angle_q, power_q, spin_q)` fills the preview buffer with the first 0.4 s, `preview_len()` points. `bot_shot()` is the solver's best first move from the current lie, packed `angle | power << 8 | spin << 14`.
- `view_ptr()`: 16 f64: x, y, vx, vy, w, phase (0 ready, 1 air, 2 ground, 3 holed), strokes, par, wind m/s, length m, cup x, sample count, hole index, holes completed, total strokes, total par. `heights_ptr()`: 160 f64 every 0.5 m; `surf_ptr()`: 160 bytes (0 fairway, 1 sand, 2 water, 3 green); `preview_ptr()`: up to 64 x,y pairs; `tele_ptr()`: clamped frames, dropped ticks, ticks, shots.
- `hash_lo()`, `hash_hi()`: the chained state hash as two u32 (JavaScript reads them signed; apply `>>> 0`). `replay_buf_ptr()`: a 512-byte buffer; `replay_encode()` writes the code of the completed holes and returns its length; `replay_verify(len)` parses the buffer and re-simulates it on a separate instance; `replay_seed()` and `replay_summary_ptr()` (holes, strokes, par) describe the last verified code.

## What the tests changed

Each line is an assumption the build started with, what a test showed, and what changed.

- **The par bot was using the walls.** Played frame by frame through the wasm, the solver holed two holes in one: one by overshooting, bouncing off the invisible far wall and dropping in from the air, one with a shot hit backwards off the near wall. Par rested on a game convention, not on golf. The solver, its robustness check and `bot_shot` now treat any shot that touches an end wall as a dead end; the player may still use the walls. Goldens and the par sweep were re-run.
- **The tunnelling test was checking a zero.** Its "at most 40 bounces per shot" read a counter that the rest rule had already reset. The counter now resets only when a shot starts.
- **The replay path had never run in the browser engine.** `check.mjs` reproduced the golden hashes but never touched the second instance that verifies replay codes. It now plays two holes, encodes them, expects a match, then changes one character and expects a mismatch.
- **Spin settings barely change carry.** The plan was that the three spin settings would differ by more than 3 m of carry at full power. Measured: 0.4-2.4 m, because the lift curve is two-thirds saturated at the lowest setting. Spin against no spin is 10 m or more, and what separates the settings is the descent angle and the roll-out. The test asserts those instead.
- **The first surface table printed the wrong velocities.** One step from just above the ground does not reach it at 30 m/s, so "after bounce" was the incoming ball. The table now steps until the first bounce.
- **A bounce exactly on a vertex could skip the next crossing.** The neighbouring segment's line can sit 1e-16 m above the ball. It was never seen in 20,000 property shots, but the sweep now tolerates a start up to 1e-9 m below the line. The goldens did not change.
- **The par target held without tuning.** The plan expected the true-size cup to push par to 4-5 and had a list of levers ready. The first 90-hole sweep gave a solver best of 1-2 with no regenerations, so no lever was pulled.

## How to run

```
cd golf
cargo test                                  # the ladder, debug
cargo test --release -- --ignored           # 30-seed par sweep and write_report (REPORT.md, the no-JS polyline)
sh build.sh                                 # wasm -> public/golf/golf.wasm, size gate, node check.mjs
UPDATE_GOLDEN=1 cargo test golden_hashes    # after an intentional physics change, then re-run build.sh
```

Requires a Rust toolchain with the `wasm32-unknown-unknown` target and node 22.

## Sources

- Biber, Jones, Champneys, Green, Szalai, "Measurements and linearized models for golf ball bounce", arXiv 2302.02758 (2023). Read directly: the rigid bounce map (eq. 5), the landing measurement used as the regression check, and the error figures quoted above.
- Bearman and Harvey (1976) and Smits and Smith, "A new aerodynamic model of a golf ball in flight" (1994): the wind-tunnel ranges and the spin-decay form were taken second-hand, from the text of US patent 10,597,154 B1 and from search summaries. The papers themselves were not read.
- Published tour carries, launch angles and spin rates (driver, 7-iron, pitching wedge) from trade-press summaries of launch-monitor averages; the wedge launch angle was not published and is an assumption (24 degrees).
- The standard Stimpmeter release speed (1.83 m/s); from the Rules of Golf, the ball (1.680 in minimum diameter, 1.620 oz maximum mass) and the hole (4.25 in).
- The fixed-step frame schedule follows the usual accumulator pattern ("Fix Your Timestep").
